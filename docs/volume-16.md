# Part I. Philosophy

## The Promise
One mission. Why this house exists. 
It exists to act as a grounded, living representation of a mind. You are building places to meet people. 

## Universal Design Rules
These apply to every room, every interaction, every future feature:
- Every room communicates one clear idea.
- Every room has a unique atmosphere.
- Every room has one signature interaction.
- Every room rewards curiosity.
- Every object exists for a reason.
- Every transition is animated.
- Every interaction respects accessibility.
- Every feature works on both desktop and mobile, even if implemented differently.
- Every visitor should feel welcomed, whether they're a gamer, a recruiter, or someone who barely touches a keyboard.

## Chapter 3. View Modes (Crucial)
**🌌 Immersive Mode**
The intended experience.
- First-person, Pointer lock, WASD, Crosshair interaction.
- Maximum immersion. Default for desktop.
- *Purpose:* Live inside the mansion.

**🏛 Explorer Mode**
- Orbit camera, Click-to-move, Free rotation.
- Better for non-gamers & accessibility. Mobile default.
- *Purpose:* Explore the mansion comfortably.

**🏗 Architect Mode (optional future)**
- Roof fades away, Dollhouse perspective, Entire floorplan visible.
- *Purpose:* Appreciate the architecture.

## Chapter 4. Transition Philosophy
Switching modes should never feel like flipping a setting. The camera should physically move.
- Walking → floating upward.
- Explorer → descending back into your body.
- **No hard cuts.**

## Chapter 5. Navigation Philosophy
- House map / Fast travel
- Animated movement (Never teleport)
- Walking should reveal something
- Returning home

## Chapter 6. Accessibility
Not a checklist. A philosophy.
- **Motion:** Reduced camera sway, Smooth movement toggle, Instant movement option, Camera speed.
- **Vision:** High contrast, Font scaling, Crosshair options, Color accessibility.
- **Controls:** Keyboard, Mouse, Controller, Touch.
- **Comfort:** Ambient volume, Motion sensitivity, Auto interactions, Skip long transitions.

## Chapter 7. Mobile Experience
Not "responsive design." Mobile is a different experience.
Desktop is: *Live in the mansion.*
Mobile is: *Carry the mansion in your pocket.*
- No virtual joysticks if possible. Tap to move. Tap objects.
- Explorer Mode by default. Simplified UI.
- Same emotional experience. Same visual quality where possible.
- The goal is not feature parity. The goal is experience parity.

## Chapter 8. Settings Philosophy
Not a boring settings menu. The settings themselves should feel like part of the mansion.
- Settings should enhance immersion, not break it (View Mode, Accessibility, Graphics, Audio, Time of Day, Weather).

# Part III. Mansion Masterplan

Every room gets exactly the same template. This is the implementation checklist.

## ROOM TEMPLATE
**Identity:** Room Name, Wing, Version, Status, Purpose
**Core Message:** What is this room trying to communicate? (One sentence)

**Desired Visitor Transformation**
- Before entering: ...
- After leaving: ...

**Emotion:** What should they feel? (No more than three. e.g. Wonder, Comfort, Focus, Nostalgia)
**Story:** Why does this room exist? Why is it part of your life?

**Architecture & Visual Language**
- Ceiling, Doors, Windows, Scale, Shape, Furniture, Layout.
- Palette, Materials, Lighting, Weather, Time of day, Particles.

**Audio**
- Ambient, Footsteps, Mechanical sounds, Silence.

**Objects**
- Every object inside. Desk, Chair, Books, Laptop.

**Interaction Matrix**
- Object -> Interaction -> Purpose -> Outcome (e.g. Telescope -> Use -> Exploration -> Opens universe)

**Signature Interaction**
- Only one. The unforgettable thing.

**Hidden Moments**
- Secrets, rare events, random occurrences that reward curiosity.

**Communication Strategy**
- How does this room communicate without relying on explanatory text?

**Room Success Metrics (Experience Metrics)**
- [ ] Did the visitor smile?
- [ ] Did they spend longer than expected?
- [ ] Did they discover something without being told?
- [ ] Did they understand what this room says about me?
- [ ] Did they want to return?
